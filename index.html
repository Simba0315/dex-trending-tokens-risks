Trending DEX tokens: how to read a +1,600% bubble without getting burned

Adam Shanin · 25 November 2026

On 25 November 2026, while most large coins were falling, the DEX view on CryptoBubbles showed 72 tokens trending on decentralised exchanges, the biggest of them up 1,602% in 24 hours. Numbers like that are what pull people into new tokens. This page explains what a trending DEX token actually is, and the five checks that separate a real market from a trap.

What "trending on a DEX" means

Decentralised exchanges such as Raydium, PumpSwap, Uniswap and PancakeSwap let anyone create a token and a trading pool in minutes, with no listing review. A token "trends" when its pool sees a burst of trades and price movement. The DEX bubble chart pulls trending pools from GeckoTerminal every five minutes and hides pools with less than $10,000 in liquidity or daily volume, which removes the worst spam but not the risk.

Why the percentages are so large

A new token often starts with a tiny pool. If a pool holds $20,000 and a few buyers add $30,000, the price can multiply many times over in an hour. The same arithmetic works in reverse: when early holders sell into that thin pool, the price can fall 90% just as fast. A +1,602% bubble says how thin the market was, not how valuable the token is.

Five checks before treating a token as real

  1. Liquidity and whether it is locked. If the pool's liquidity can be withdrawn by the creator at any time, the "rug pull" is one transaction away.
  2. Holder distribution. If a handful of wallets hold most of the supply, they decide the price. Tools that map wallets show whether those holders are linked to each other.
  3. The contract. Some tokens are "honeypots": anyone can buy, but the contract blocks selling. Check that the contract is verified and that other wallets have actually sold.
  4. The name. Tokens often copy the name or ticker of a famous company or project. On the same day the trending list included tokens named after well-known listed companies that have nothing to do with those firms.
  5. Age and volume over time. A pool that is a few hours old with all its volume in one spike is a different thing from one that has traded steadily for months.

Where it fits in reading the market

The main bubble chart shows established coins ranked by market cap; the DEX view shows the newest and most speculative corner of the market. Looking at both side by side is useful context: on a day when only 8 of the 100 largest coins were up and the Fear and Greed Index read 13, a list of tokens up hundreds of percent says more about speculation than about the market's direction.
